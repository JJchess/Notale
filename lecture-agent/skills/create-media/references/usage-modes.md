# Media purposes and placements

## Purpose: why the page uses media

- `evidence`: the asset itself is observable evidence. Require a faithful subject, provenance, and a caption when interpretation could be ambiguous.
- `explanatory`: the asset explains an object, mechanism, or abstract metaphor. State exactly what correspondence the learner should read.
- `narrative`: the asset establishes a person, event, place, problem, or situation needed by the lesson.
- `atmospheric`: the asset establishes mood, rhythm, or a deck-wide motif. It carries no factual burden.

## Placement: how the asset enters the page

- `illustration`: use as a main or supporting visual. Declare subject, focal point, crop intent, `contain|cover`, and its relationship to the claim.
- `decoration`: use for textures, edge motifs, corner marks, or non-semantic objects. Keep it out of reading order, below the content hierarchy, and away from controls.
- `background`: use for full-page or regional scenery and texture. Declare focal point, safe zone, overlay, and contrast target. Keep critical text out of the pixels and pair the background with native content blocks.

A poster page is a `background` visual layer plus native text. A large independent image is an `illustration`.
